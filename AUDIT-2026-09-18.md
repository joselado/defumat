# What twenty parallel audits found, 2026-09-18

## What this is

Twenty read-only agents were run over the package, one per class of defect, and each
dimension's findings were then handed to a separate agent told to refute them. The
dimensions are this project's own recurring traps rather than a generic bug taxonomy,
because a generic list rediscovers what `PLAN.md` has already measured. 38 agents, 26
minutes, 1279 tool calls, no agent errors.

48 claims were made and 3 were refuted, leaving **45**: 40 confirmed and 5 plausible.

**Read that refutation rate as a warning about the audit and not as a compliment to
it.** A verification pass that kills 3 of 48 is either looking at a codebase whose
defects are unusually easy to establish, or is not adversarial enough to surprise
itself, and this audit cannot tell you which. Two dimensions returned nothing at all
(the clamp tangents and the derivative memory), and one had both its claims refuted
(rule D4), which is the only direct evidence here that the refuters were doing
anything. Nothing below was reproduced by running the code, with one exception noted
in its own entry, so every entry is a claim about what the source says rather than a
measurement of what it computes. That is the opposite of this project's standard and
it is why none of this belongs in `PLAN.md` until it has a number.

The ordering is `CLAUDE.md`'s: a silent wrong answer outranks a missing feature, which
outranks an inconvenience.

**Provenance, because none of it is reproducible from this file alone.** The run was
`Workflow` with twenty audit agents pipelined into twenty refuters, on commit `e2d4c5e`,
2026-09-18. The per-agent transcripts and the raw JSON live under the session's own
directory and in `/tmp`, neither of which survives, so **this file is the only durable
record** and nothing below can be traced back further than what is written here. If an
entry's file and line no longer contain what it describes, re-grep rather than trusting
it: the line numbers are as of `e2d4c5e`.

### wrong-answer.1 `defumat/hubbard/projectors.py:72` -- abs-at-zero

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

**The claim.** `O^{-1/2}` is built by `jnp.linalg.eigh`, whose reverse-mode rule carries `1/(lambda_i - lambda_j)`, and the atomic-orbital overlap has eigenvalue degeneracies that site symmetry forces to zero. The DFT+U force and stress differentiate straight through it, so a `Hubbard_projectors = 'ortho-atomic'` (or `'norm-atomic'`) force or stress is silently wrong where the degeneracy is broken only by round-off. The matrix function `O^{-1/2}` is itself perfectly smooth at a degeneracy; it is the eigendecomposition route that is not, so nothing in the physics warns of it.

**What goes wrong.** The sharpest case is the stress, which has neither escape a force has (a force at a symmetric geometry is zero by symmetry and `symvector` projects a spurious part away, and a displaced geometry lifts the degeneracy physically). `Calculator.get_stress()` on `tests/data/qe/ni-ldau-ortho.in`'s fcc nickel or `tests/data/qe/pt-yukawa.in`'s platinum: the cell keeps its cubic symmetry at every volume, so `O` keeps exact-to-round-off degeneracies at the k-points of even the shifted `4 4 4 1 1 1` grid, the strain perturbation is generic inside the degenerate block, and cubic symmetrisation of the tensor keeps the erroneous isotropic part. The Hubbard contribution to the stress is then wrong by an O(1) fraction of itself, with no error raised. Measured at the source: on the real overlaps of a silicon ortho-atomic run the tangent of `lowdin_transform` is wrong by 42 per cent of its largest element at Gamma and 33 per cent at (1/8,1/8,1/8), against 3.1e-10 at a generic k-point. The same applies to `Calculator.get_forces()` wherever a correlated atom keeps its site symmetry while other atoms carry the force. Nothing detects it: the trace of the erroneous derivative is right, so `test_occupation_matrix_traces` and every energy assertion pass, and the only reference force and stress comparisons in the suite use `HUBBARD {atomic}`, which never reaches the eigh.

**What the verifier found.** The code reads as claimed and I measured the defect on the repository's own construction path rather than on a synthetic matrix. Line 72-74 is `values, vectors = jnp.linalg.eigh(overlap)` followed by `(vectors * jax.lax.rsqrt(values)) @ vectors.conj().T`, with no `custom_jvp` anywhere near it (the only `custom_jvp`s in the package are in `xc/mgga.py`, `scf/residual.py`, `scf/occupations.py`, `pseudo/augmentation.py`). It is reached from `build_atomic_projectors.one_kpoint` (line 298) whenever `kind in ('ortho-atomic','norm-atomic')` (line 287), and `scf/driver.py:2440` (`at_positions`) and `:2676`/`:2831` (`at_strain`, `at_kpoints`) rebuild `wfcU` through it, so it is on both the force and the stress tape. `forces/energy.py:478-485` and `forces/analytic.py:85-95` both say in as many words that this autodiff path is where `force_hub`'s `d(O^{-1/2})/dtau` is supposed to come from, and `PLAN.md`'s own rule D4 (line 17418) says "Do not differentiate through the diagonalization" because the derivative is singular at degeneracies "which crystals have everywhere by symmetry" -- line 72 is that rule broken. I captured the actual `O` handed to line 72 by instrumenting `lowdin_transform` (scratchpad only, unmodified tree, no test run) for a two-atom silicon cell with `HUBBARD ortho-atomic` on Si-3p at three k-points. At Gamma the 8x8 overlap has two threefold-degenerate eigenvalues with gaps of 0.0, 2.8e-16 and 1.1e-16; at (1/8,1/8,1/8) -- the point a *shifted* 4x4x4 fcc grid actually contains, little group C3v with a two-dimensional irrep -- there is a bit-exact degenerate pair. Differentiating the exact two lines at 72-73 with a generic Hermitian tangent and comparing against a central difference of `scipy.linalg.fractional_matrix_power(O, -0.5)` (a primal-only reference, so it cannot be poisoned by the eigh derivative) gives a maximum absolute error of 2.10 on a derivative whose largest element is 5.04 at Gamma (42 per cent) and 1.15 on 3.49 at the Lambda point (33 per cent), while a generic k-point agrees to 3.1e-10 -- which is what validates the transpose convention and the finite-difference step. Three corrections to the original claim. The result is finite, not NaN: `jax._src.lax.linalg._eigh_jvp_rule` builds `Fmat = 1/(eye + w_j - w_i) - eye`, which would be `inf` at bit-exact equality, but `jnp.linalg.eigh` leaves a ~1e-16 residual gap where `np.linalg.eigvalsh` did not, so the outcome is a wrong number of order tens of per cent rather than a crash. The shifted `4 4 4 1 1 1` grid of `ni-ldau-ortho.in` is *not* the escape the original allowed it -- it is exposed too, and is unexposed only because nothing in the suite differentiates it. And the error is not confined to a traceless rotation: the worst Lambda element was the diagonal [5,5] (AD -1.69 against -0.57), the error being off-diagonal in `O`'s eigenbasis but landing on diagonal entries in the atomic-orbital basis. What is not measured is the size on a converged force or stress, and I did not run an SCF for it; the link is structural: `wfcU = O^{-1/2} S phi` feeds `ns` feeds `E_U`, the selected Hubbard columns (Si-3p, Ni-3d) are exactly the degenerate blocks, and the error matrix is Hermitian in that block rather than anti-Hermitian, so both `Tr[ns]` and `Tr[ns^2]` move. Nothing covers the differentiated path: `tests/regression/test_ldau.py:241 test_forces` is pinned to `pw_lda+U/lda+U_force.in`, whose card is `HUBBARD {atomic}`; `tests/data/qe/ni-ldau-stress.in` is `HUBBARD {atomic}`; `tests/unit/test_hubbard.py:281` exercises `lowdin_transform` on a random (non-degenerate) overlap and never differentiates it; and `stress/energy.py`'s refusals cover only spirals and magnetic fields.

### wrong-answer.2 `defumat/workflows/magnons.py:259` -- kset-spin

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

**The claim.** `run_spin_susceptibility` puts a caller-supplied `kpoints` straight onto the system with `eqx.tree_at` and never calls `for_spin`, so on the `nspin = 2` run it requires the weights arrive summing to 2 instead of 1 and every k-integral in the transverse susceptibility is exactly doubled.

**What goes wrong.** `calc.get_spin_susceptibility(q, frequencies, kpoints=KPoints.automatic((8,8,8),(0,0,0),cell))` on a gapped ferromagnet -- the h-fcc case the repository already uses -- or the same raw mesh passed to `run_spin_susceptibility` directly. The mesh's weights sum to 2 where an nspin = 2 run needs 1, so with `occupations = 'fixed'` the occupation factors saturate, `wg = w_k` exactly, and chi_0 comes back exactly 2x. `goldstone_residual` (spinkernel.py:264) compares that against a target built from the SCF density, which is unchanged, so it returns |2m - m|/|m| ~ 1.0, 100 per cent, where its own docstring quotes 0.50 to 8 per cent converged and predicts this outright: 'a factor of two in the response's normalisation fails it by a factor of two'. The magnon energy from `_locate` is wrong with it, since the pole condition becomes `1 - 2 X_0 F` singular, and `enhancement` is doubled so `.unstable` fires wherever the true enhancement exceeds 0.5. With smearing on a metal the Fermi solve additionally lands at the energy holding nelec/2 electrons, so f_up - f_dn is wrong as well as the prefactor. The measured sibling of this defect one workflow over put fcc nickel's plasma frequency at 13.11 eV instead of 0.60 (PLAN.md:8016-8026).

**What the verifier found.** I read the whole chain and there is no guard anywhere below the substitution. magnons.py:258-259 is `if kpoints is not None: system = eqx.tree_at(lambda s: s.kpoints, system, kpoints)` with no `for_spin`, and :265-268 calls `fixed_density_states(system, pseudos, density, nbnd=..., conv_thr=..., k_batch=...)` with no `kpoints=`, so the for_spin boundary at nscf.py:135-152 -- whose own comment claims it 'covers every caller that builds its own set' -- never fires. Every sibling does apply it at the identical point (conductivity.py:107, shg.py:84, photocurrent.py:80, nesting.py:130, transport.py:553, ultracell/driver.py:891, calculator.py:1741 in `with_kpoints`). `KPoints.from_crystal` runs `_normalise` (kpoints.py:563-569), which multiplies by DEGSPIN, and `spin_normalized` defaults to False (kpoints.py:388), so a set from a bare constructor such as `KPoints.automatic(grid, shift, cell)` carries weights summing to 2. `require_a_transverse_regime` (spinchi0.py:187-296) pins the run to nspin = 2 and its only k-set test, :289-290, is `np.ptp(weights) > 1e-8 * max` -- a uniformity test that a uniform factor of two passes. `Calculation.occupations` (driver.py:4283-4290) reads `self.system.kpoints.weights` raw, `_fixed_occupations_spin` fills bands at those weights with no renormalisation, and `transverse_response` (spinchi0.py:454-455, :510-512) feeds `wg` straight into the chi_0 sum; nothing in magnons.py:300-322 divides by a weight total. I grepped `spin_normalized`, `weights.sum()` and `weights).sum()` across scf/driver.py and the whole of tddft/ and found nothing, so no downstream boundary rescues it. `kpoints` is a named parameter of `run_spin_susceptibility` (magnons.py:190) and `_defaults_for` merges `{**shared, **options}` (calculator.py:1843) with the caller's options honoured verbatim, so it is reachable as `calc.get_spin_susceptibility(q, w, kpoints=...)`. What limits the scope, and belongs in the scenario rather than refuting it: `for_spin` is idempotent through the flag, so a set from `denser_grid` or from `calc.system.kpoints` passes through untouched -- the trap needs a set from a bare constructor, which is exactly what the docstring's 'whole k-grid, nosym, noinv' requirement steers a caller toward. No test, notebook or docs snippet passes `kpoints=` to either entry point, and the parameter is absent from the docstring's Args list, so nothing currently exercises it and nothing warns.

### wrong-answer.3 `defumat/workflows/magnons.py:418` -- kset-spin

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

**The claim.** The same missing `for_spin` in `run_magnon_dispersion`, where `goldstone_correction = True` makes it undetectable: a factor of two in the weights is exactly q-independent, so the correction absorbs it and the diagnostic that would have caught it reports a clean dispersion.

**What goes wrong.** `calc.get_magnon_dispersion(qpath, frequencies, kpoints=<a raw denser mesh>)` on a ferromagnet, in two branches. With the default `goldstone_correction = False`: chi_0 is doubled for the reason in the previous finding, the pole condition becomes `1 - 2 X_0 F` singular, omega(q) is wrong at every q, and `enhancement` is doubled so `.unstable` reports an instability wherever the true enhancement exceeds 0.5. That is the default and it is the wrong-answer case. With `goldstone_correction = True`: the factor is exactly q-independent, `scale = 1/lambda_max(2 X_0 F)` halves, `X_0 * scale * F` is invariant, and omega(q) and `.enhancements` come back correct -- the readable symptoms are `kernel_scale` sitting near half its honest value, which the docstring presents as a truncation measure so a reader attributes it to `nbnd` or `ecut_response`, and `chi`/`spectral` doubled in magnitude. The honesty check is not fully disabled there: `.goldstone` still reads about 1.0 whenever Gamma is on the path, and is `None` only for a path that avoids it.

**What the verifier found.** magnons.py:417-422 is the identical substitution -- `eqx.tree_at(lambda s: s.kpoints, system, kpoints)`, then `require_a_transverse_regime`, then `fixed_density_states(..., nbnd=, conv_thr=, k_batch=)` with no `kpoints=` and no `for_spin` -- so it is the same defect at a second site, and every element of the chain I verified for the first claim applies unchanged. The algebra of the correction is as claimed: `scale = 1/uniform.enhancement` (magnons.py:427-435) and `enhancement` is `lambda_max(x0[0] @ kernel)` (magnons.py:304-306), so doubling X_0 halves the scale and the product `X_0 * scale * F` that both the Dyson solve (:300) and `_locate`'s crossing condition are built from is exactly invariant. omega(q) and `.enhancements` therefore come back unchanged, which is the claim's substance. Its detectability half is overstated and I have corrected the scenario: `MagnonDispersion.goldstone` is `next((r.goldstone for r in results if r.goldstone is not None), None)` (magnons.py:461-462) over the per-q runs, and `run_spin_susceptibility` sets `goldstone = bool(np.all(np.abs(q) < 1e-10))` by default (magnons.py:271-272), so any path through Gamma -- the notebook's `[(0,0,0), (0,0.25,0.25)]` included -- still reports a residual near 1.0. Only a path that misses Gamma is silent. What is not silenced by the correction in any case is `kernel_scale`, reading about half its honest value, and `chi`/`spectral`, doubled in magnitude.

### wrong-answer.4 `defumat/response/conductivity.py:430` -- symmetrisation

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

**The claim.** Every "is this k-set a wedge" guard in the package decides by looking for a spread in the k-weights, and a symmetry-reduced *shifted* Monkhorst-Pack grid has exactly uniform weights whenever the group acts freely on it -- which is what a shift arranges. The guard returns False on a k-set that is half or a quarter of the zone, so the refusal that protects an axial or rank-3 polar wedge sum never fires and the sum is taken over the wedge alone with nothing symmetrising it.

**What goes wrong.** Run any nonmagnetic low-symmetry (P1) cell with `K_POINTS automatic 4 4 4 1 1 1` and ask for the optical conductivity. Time reversal alone reduces the shifted grid to one member of each {k, -k} pair at weight 2, ptp = 0.0, so the guard at :406 passes and the sum runs over half the zone. The Berry-curvature summand of the antisymmetric part of sigma_ab is odd under k -> -k, so the true full-zone value is zero by time reversal while the half-zone sum at weight 2 comes out at full size: a spurious anomalous Hall conductivity and Kerr angle on a crystal that forces both to zero, which is the module's headline quantity. The general form is the claim's: with C2z in the group the components of sigma_ab odd under C2z (xz, zx, yz, zy) come out the size of sigma_xx where symmetry forces zero, and for chi_0 the head G = G' = 0 survives while the wings and body do not, so eps_M is right without local fields and wrong with them -- the shape of error that reads as a converged spectrum.

**What the verifier found.** I read the guard and reproduced the measurement. `_kpoints_are_reduced` (conductivity.py:427-430) is `np.ptp(weights) > 1e-8 * max|weights|` and nothing else, and `irreducible_wedge` (system/kpoints.py:159-164) sets each kept point's weight to its orbit multiplicity, so a group that acts freely leaves the weights bit-identical. Calling `irreducible_wedge((4,4,4), shift, ...)` directly: rotations {E} with time reversal gives nk = 32 of 64 with ptp = 0.0 exactly at shift (1,1,1) against 36 points and ptp = 0.015625 at shift (0,0,0); {E, C2z} with time reversal gives 16 of 64, ptp = 0.0; the magnetic {E, T.C2z} (t_rev = [0,1], time_reversal = False) gives 32 of 64, ptp = 0.0. That is the ordinary `K_POINTS automatic 4 4 4 1 1 1`, reached live -- builder.py:2152-2157 hands `rotations`/`time_reversal`/`t_rev` straight to `KPoints.automatic`, which takes the `irreducible_wedge` branch for any non-empty rotation list, and the identity is always in the group, so even a P1 cell reduces by time reversal. The same measurement falsifies the one place the hole is named and dismissed (workflows/stm.py:589-593, "it is the *reduction* that varies the weights on every real k-set"): the reduction of the most ordinary shifted grid QE produces varies them not at all. Nothing downstream rescues it -- `require_a_conductivity_regime` has only the spiral refusal ahead of this one, the assembly sums `wk` at conductivity.py:562 with no symmetrisation, and chi0.py's own docstring (:431) says "Nothing is symmetrised". The five copies (tddft/chi0.py:368-380, response/photocurrent.py:599-609 reached by shg.py through `require_a_velocity_sum_regime`, tddft/spinchi0.py:290, workflows/transport.py:1185, workflows/stm.py:596) share the test and the hole. `nosym = .true.` is the documented escape and is genuinely safe, which is why no committed test trips this.

### wrong-answer.5 `defumat/response/effmass.py:506` -- stencil

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

**The claim.** The centre-free second difference cancels the plane-wave basis offset only when every stencil point holds the same number of plane waves. That is true at Gamma and false at any other centre, so at a conduction-band minimum the diagonal curvature inherits a shell-crossing offset divided by 3h^2 -- the same error-grows-as-h-shrinks signature the routine was written to cure, moved from the centre out to the stencil radii. Nothing in the code checks the counts, the Richardson step does not remove it, and neither reported diagnostic can see it.

**What goes wrong.** Two-atom silicon, alat = 10.2, ecutwfc = 30, mass at L, kpoint = (0.5, 0.5, 0.5) crystal, method = "eigenvalue", defaults otherwise (delta = 0.025, richardson = True). Line 468 halves delta, so the coarse stencil is h = 0.0125. Verified counts: centre 754, near (+-h) 752 on every axis, far (+-2h) 744 on every axis. The far pair therefore sits in a basis eight plane waves smaller than the near pair and is variationally high by a fixed offset delta_8, the numerator (far - near) at line 506 inherits +2 delta_8, and the diagonal curvature inherits +2 delta_8 / (3 h^2) on all three entries. Scaling delta_8 from the module's own Gamma measurement of 1.2e-6 Ry for an eight-plane-wave shell gives about +5.1e-3 Ry bohr^2, i.e. about +2.6e-3 in 1/m_e -- roughly 0.26 per cent on a band with 1/m ~ 1 and several per cent on a flat band, with the inverse mass too large and the mass too small. At the Richardson half-step the verified counts are 754 near against 752 far, a two-plane-wave shell, so the same offset-over-h^2 form recurs at a comparable size and (4 M(h/2) - M(h))/3 returns it rather than removing it, while truncation = |M(h/2) - M(h)|/3 reads small and reports the error as absent. The same mismatch occurs at silicon's real conduction minimum: at (0.85, 0, 0) in 2pi/alat, alat = 10.2, the axis-0 near points hold 741 and 745 against far points at 741 and 749 (verified), and at alat = 10.26 the near points hold 749 and 751 against far points at 745, 751 and 753. The absolute size is set by the shell rather than by the band, so it is worst exactly where a flat band is wanted.

**What the verifier found.** I reproduced the counts independently and they come out exactly as claimed. Calibration first: on the fcc ibrav=2 cell with defumat's own rule from basis/planewaves.py:158-166 (|k+G|^2 <= ecutwfc with the 1e-8 snap), alat = 10.26 and ecutwfc = 30 give 725 plane waves at Gamma and 733 at every point of both stencils, which is the docstring's own measurement at line 427-428 and PLAN.md P48a, so the counting model is the code's. At L = (0.5,0.5,0.5) crystal, alat = 10.2, ecutwfc = 30: centre 754, the four near points (+-0.0125) 752 on every axis, the four far points (+-0.025) 744 on every axis. At the Richardson half-step the near points (+-0.00625) hold 754 and the far ones (+-0.0125) hold 752. So the premise the routine rests on, stated verbatim at line 453-454 ("Every point in both is displaced, so the basis-set offset is common to all of them and cancels"), is a Gamma statement and is false at the very centre the public docstring advertises at line 284-285 ("(0.5, 0.5, 0.5) for L on an fcc cell"). The spheres really are per k-point: _stencil_kpoints builds a KPoints, fixed_density_bands goes through build_plane_wave_basis (scf/driver.py:1547, :2750), and planewaves.select gives each k its own sphere padded to a common npwx. There is no npw or count check anywhere in effmass.py, no refusal of a non-Gamma centre in docs/features.tex's amber box (which refuses only a degenerate multiplet and a spin spiral), and every numerical test in tests/unit/test_effective_mass.py runs at (0,0,0) (lines 63, 161, 189), where the counts are uniform. The decisive point against calling this an already-handled case: the project has measured this exact mechanism in the *arm* rather than the centre and named the cure. PLAN.md:8555-8561 records a validating difference that got 158 plane waves at k and k - delta but 157 at k + delta on a generic AlAs point, with the relative disagreement *growing* as the step shrank (0.25, 0.28, 0.49 at delta = 4e-3, 2e-3, 1e-3), and states "Freezing the sphere across the stencil is both the cure and the correct comparison". effmass.py does not freeze the sphere. So the mechanism is measured in this project rather than argued, and the fix exists elsewhere in the codebase and is not used here. What I did not verify is the coefficient: the 1.2e-6 Ry offset per eight-plane-wave shell was measured at Gamma and is transferred to L, so the quoted 2.6e-3 in 1/m_e is an estimate, not a measurement. The qualitative conclusion does not depend on it -- the numerator inherits twice a fixed basis-offset difference divided by 3h^2, which is not O(h^2), so the Richardson combination (4 M(h/2) - M(h))/3 does not remove it and EffectiveMass.truncation, being |M(h/2) - M(h)|/3, under-reports it whatever the ratio of the two shells' offsets turns out to be. _asymmetry cannot see it either, since the error is equal on all three diagonal entries at L by symmetry.

### wrong-answer.6 `defumat/scf/density.py:149` -- gamma-storage

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

**The claim.** The meta-GGA kinetic energy density tau is built with the whole-sphere transform g_to_r applied to half-sphere-stored states, with no conjugate fill and no refusal anywhere on the path.

**What goes wrong.** A norm-conserving dataset, nosym = .true., K_POINTS gamma, input_dft = 'tb09' (or 'bj06'). The run completes with no warning and no refusal and returns a plausible tau. Writing h(r) = sum_{G in half} i G c_G e^{iGr}, the true gradient of the real state is 2 Re h, so the code forms |h|^2 = (Re h)^2 + (Im h)^2 where the physics is 4 (Re h)^2. The error is not a uniform factor: pointwise tau loses the oscillation entirely, and only its *integral* is a clean factor of two, since the half sphere holds no (G, -G) pair, so int tau_computed = (1/2) int tau_true exactly. rho is right, because it takes the g_to_r_gamma branch, so in tb09_potential (xc/mgga.py:288-320) the term v_x = c v_x^BR + (3c-2)/pi sqrt(5/12) sqrt(2 tau/rho) is wrong by a spatially varying amount whose integrated size is 1/sqrt(2) = 0.707, a 29 per cent error in the one term a potential-only meta-GGA exists for, and D = 2 tau - |grad rho|^2/(4 rho) in the Becke-Roussel part (mgga.py:266) is wrong by more, since only one of its two terms is affected. docs/features.tex:495-497 records P68's gamma validation as covering LDA, PBE, LSDA and PBE+LSDA only, and tests/regression/test_gamma_only.py contains no meta-GGA case.

**What the verifier found.** Read the whole path. `band_kinetic_density` (density.py:149) forms `components = 1j * kplusg.T * state` and calls `g_to_r(components, fft_index, grid)` -- the whole-sphere transform -- and its signature has no `fft_index_minus` parameter at all, unlike `band_density` (density.py:60), which branches to `g_to_r_gamma` when one is passed. `Calculation.kinetic_energy_density` (driver.py:3642-3645) calls `_kinetic_of_bands(wavefunctions, self.fft_index, smooth.grid, weights, self.system.cell, self.kplusg, self.k_batch)` with no `fft_index_minus`, against driver.py:3596-3598 where the density's own call passes `fft_index_minus=self.fft_index_minus`. ### drift.1 `defumat/response/sternheimer.py:555` -- dtypes

**Verdict: PLAUSIBLE.**

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

**The claim.** `SCFResult.density` always keeps its `(nspin_mag, ...)` leading axis, including at one channel, but two docstrings state the opposite convention and one call site is written against the docstring instead of against the code.

**What goes wrong.** `torque_of_result` at `result.nspin == 1` builds a `(1, 1, n1, n2, n3)` density and runs the whole Hartree and exchange-correlation build on it before the call is refused -- wasted work and a dead branch, but the same refusal an unpolarized call gets anyway, so no number moves. The cost is carried by the record: driver.py:1193, workflows/dos.py:83 and CLAUDE.md all state that `SCFResult` squeezes the density's spin axis at one channel when it does not, and `spin_torque.py:174` is the first consumer written to that statement. **What the verifier found.** The code keeps the axis. `SCFResult.density` is annotated `(nspin, n1, n2, n3)` at driver.py:1101 and is filled with `density=rho` at driver.py:5930 with no squeeze, where `eigenvalues` and `occupations` two lines above are squeezed explicitly; `SCFResult.total_density` calls `total_charge`, which reads `rho_g.shape[0]` as the channel count (scf/potential.py:98-106) and would sum the wrong axis on a squeezed array; `workflows/ultracell.py:166` reads `int(np.asarray(result.density).shape[0])` as `nspin_mag` and indexes `density[0]` below it. Three places say the opposite: driver.py:1193, on `ns`, says the spin axis is kept there "unlike the density's"; workflows/dos.py:82-83 says the DOS axis is squeezed "the same convention SCFResult uses for the density"; and CLAUDE.md's own conventions section says the result objects "drop that axis when nspin = 1". `spin_torque.py:174` is written against that record. But the failure the original claim attached to it does not follow, and I am correcting it: `exchange_torque`'s guard (spin_torque.py:118) reads `density.shape[0]`, which is 1 with or without the `[None]`, so an unpolarized `get_exchange_torque()` raises the identical ValueError either way; `v_of_rho` already promotes a bare 3-D grid itself (potential.py:706-710), and `r_to_g`/`gather_from_box` work on `axes=(-3, -2, -1)` with `box.shape[:-3] + (-1,)` (basis/fft.py:51-68), so the spurious axis rides through without a crash. The live defect is the record, not the number.

### drift.15 `defumat/scf/driver.py:4936` -- continuation

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

**The claim.** The kinetic energy density is carried on a shape test alone, with no reference to the magnetization mode the continuation just resolved, so a `magnetization='none'` or `'seed'` continuation starts a meta-GGA run from a spin-polarized `tau` on a density whose magnetization was deliberately thrown away.

**What goes wrong.** A `tb09` or `bj06` run at `nspin = 2` converged ferromagnetic, continued into the same regime with `magnetization='none'` -- the mode documented as 'the only way to hand the target a state it can converge away from without the seed choosing the answer'. `state.density` comes back with both channels equal, `source_tau` has shape (2, nx, ny, nz) which equals the expected shape, so the converged spin-split `tau` is carried. The Becke-Roussel/TB09 potential is a function of `tau_s` and `rho_s` per channel, so iteration 1 diagonalises two different Hamiltonians on a spin-degenerate density: the state 'none' promises is not stationary and the run is pushed off it in the source's direction rather than being left free to choose.

**What the verifier found.** The block at driver.py:4929-4936 is exactly as quoted: `source_tau = getattr(starting_from, 'tau', None)`, then `expected = tuple(np.shape(calculation.starting_density()))` and `if tuple(np.shape(source_tau)) == expected: starting_tau = source_tau`. `state`, built ten lines earlier with `state.magnetization` already resolved to 'carry'/'seed'/'none', is never read here, and the comment above the block explains only the reshape case. For a 2 -> 2 continuation the shapes match, so the converged spin-split `tau` is carried; `run_scf` then takes the `elif starting_tau is not None` branch at driver.py:5190-5191 and `tau_state` feeds `calculation.potential(rho, field_scale, field, tau=tau_state)` at driver.py:5286. The fallback the code skips, `_starting_tau(rho, calculation)` at driver.py:5193, is built from the depolarized `rho` and would have been spin-degenerate, which is what makes this a defect rather than a trade-off. Collinear and noncollinear meta-GGA are supported -- `_require_meta_supported` (driver.py:1907) refuses only plain ultrasoft and spirals -- so the combination is reachable. The bound the claim states is right and is why this is drift rather than wrong-answer: `tau` is not mixed and is rebuilt from the states at driver.py:5394-5395 and reassigned at 5687-5696, so the inconsistency lives for one iteration, and the size of the resulting eigenvalue splitting is unmeasured.

### drift.16 `defumat/system/builder.py:1569` -- input-defaults

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

**The claim.** The A/B/C crystallographic form is read only when celldm(1) is zero, so an input giving both celldm and A is silently resolved in favour of celldm; QE stops on that pair. The sibling case is CELL_PARAMETERS in bohr or angstrom together with celldm(1) or A, which QE also refuses and which builder.py:1599-1602 accepts, keeping celldm[0] as the alat that then scales ATOMIC_POSITIONS alat.

**What goes wrong.** An input carrying both spellings -- a complete celldm plus a leftover `A = 5.43, B = 5.43, C = 5.43` from a half-finished conversion, the two disagreeing by the 1.8897 bohr-per-angstrom factor whenever the conversion was forgotten -- is refused by pw.x ('do not specify both celldm and a,b,c!') and accepted here, silently taking celldm and dropping A, B, C: a converged energy for whichever of the two lattice constants the user did not mean. The sibling is `CELL_PARAMETERS bohr` (or angstrom) with a nonzero celldm(1): QE stops with 'lattice parameter specified twice', while here the vectors are taken in bohr and alat is set to celldm(1) rather than to the length of the first vector, so every `ATOMIC_POSITIONS alat` coordinate is scaled by a lattice parameter that the cell itself does not come from.

**What the verifier found.** builder.py:1567-1579 reads `if celldm[0] == 0.0 and a is not None: celldm = celldm_from_abc(...)`, so a complete celldm wins over A/B/C with no diagnostic, and builder.py:1599-1602 returns Cell.from_vectors(vectors, alat=celldm[0] or None) for the bohr and angstrom branches without testing celldm at all. One half of the claim's scenario is wrong and I have dropped it: the orthorhombic ibrav = 8 case is *not* silent, because Cell.from_ibrav guards it -- cell.py:143 `_require(2, celldm[1] > 0.0)` raises on the celldm(2) = 0 that the skipped conversion leaves.

### smell.1 `defumat/response/elastic.py:171` -- asarray-in-grad

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

**The claim.** `nocc = np.asarray(response.dpsi[0, 0]).shape[2]` materializes a whole wavefunction-sized JAX array on the host to read one integer, where `response.dpsi[0, 0].shape[2]` gives the same number with no device access.

**What goes wrong.** A norm-conserving slab with nspin=1, nk=16, nbnd_occ=100 and npwx=40000 run on GPU: `Calculator.get_elastic_constants()` transfers 16*100*40000*16 = 1.02 GB of complex128 from device to host to obtain the integer 100, and that host copy is then held on the array for the lifetime of the calculator's cached strain response rather than freed. On the CPU backend the same line costs approximately nothing, because the numpy array aliases the device buffer, which is why the committed silicon cells have never shown it. No number comes out wrong on either platform: the transfer happens before any `jvp` and every tangent at lines 192-196 goes through `jnp.asarray`.

**What the verifier found.** Line 171 reads exactly as claimed. `StrainResponse.dpsi` is a `(3, 3)` numpy object array (`strain.py:478`) whose entries are the solver's `dpsi = jnp.stack(blocks)` (`sternheimer.py:622`), shape `(nspin, nk, nocc, npwx)`, so the entry is a device array and `np.asarray` on it is a materialization rather than a view. `_require_one_spin_channel` pins `nspin = 1`, so axis 2 is the right one and `.shape[2]` on the JAX array alone would do; `response.deigenvalues` is already a host array (`strain.py:630`, `np.asarray(jnp.stack(blocks))`) of shape `(3, 3, nspin, nk, nocc)`, so `response.deigenvalues.shape[-1]` is a second free route to the same integer. The adversarial checks all come back clean rather than refuting: both callers (`calculator.py:948`, `electrostriction.py:971`) hand in a converged ground state and are outside any trace, so this never meets a tracer and cannot raise; the differentiated path two lines down goes through `jnp.asarray` (line 192), so no gradient is dropped and no number is wrong; and this is the only `np.asarray` applied to a `dpsi` anywhere in the package. What the original claim overstates is the cost rather than the reading: the sync is a non-event, since `_self_consistent_response` reads its convergence history on the host every iteration and nothing is left pending; on the CPU backend, which is the only platform here, JAX aliases the single-device buffer for `np.asarray` (it marks the result non-writeable for that reason) so the copy is likely zero bytes; and on GPU, which this project declares as the target, the copy is not transient but retained, because in JAX's current `ArrayImpl` the host array is cached in `_npy_value` for the life of the array, and `response.dpsi[0, 0]` lives as long as the calculator's cached `_strain_response`.
