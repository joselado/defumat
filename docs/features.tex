\section{pypresso: what it computes, and how to ask for
it}\label{pypresso-what-it-computes-and-how-to-ask-for-it}

User documentation for the feature set. \texttt{README.md} is the short
tour and the quickstart this is the reference --- every capability, the
entry point that reaches it, what it was validated against, and
\textbf{what it refuses}.

Two conventions run through the whole document and are worth reading
first.

\textbf{Everything is validated against Quantum ESPRESSO on the same
input}, and the agreement is quoted per feature below rather than
claimed in general. Where a number is missing it is because the
comparison does not exist, and that is said rather than hidden.

\textbf{Anything not implemented is refused by name}, with an error that
says what is missing --- never silently approximated or replaced by
something close. The "Refuses" lines below are as much a part of the
documentation as the features: they are the promise that a run which
starts is a run whose physics is there.

\textbf{One equation is written down and the rest are derived from it.}
Almost everything below is a derivative of the total energy, taken by
the compiler rather than by hand:

\[
E[\{\psi\}, \tau, \varepsilon] = T_s + E_{\rm H} + E_{xc} + E_{\rm loc} + E_{\rm nl} + E_{\rm Ewald}
\]

\begin{longtable}[]{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule\noalign{}
quantity & what it is & how it is obtained \\
\midrule\noalign{}
\endhead
\bottomrule\noalign{}
\endlastfoot
\(v_{xc}(\mathbf r)\) & \(\delta E_{xc}/\delta n\) & \texttt{jax.grad}
of the energy density \\
\(\mathbf F_I\) & \(-\partial E/\partial \boldsymbol\tau_I\) &
\texttt{jax.grad} at frozen \(\psi\) \\
\(\sigma_{ij}\) & \(-\Omega^{-1} \partial E/\partial \varepsilon_{ij}\)
& \texttt{jax.grad} along a strain \\
\(C_{I\alpha,J\beta}\) &
\(\partial^2 E/\partial\tau_{I\alpha}\partial\tau_{J\beta}\) &
\texttt{jvp} of the force \\
\(Z^{\ast}_{I,\alpha\beta}\) &
\(\partial F_{I\alpha}/\partial \mathcal E_\beta\) & \texttt{jvp} of the
force along the field response \\
\(\partial\epsilon_{ij}/\partial\tau\) & Raman & \texttt{jvp} of the
second-order energy \\
\end{longtable}

The "frozen \(\psi\)" is what makes this exact rather than approximate:
at self-consistency the energy is stationary with respect to the
wavefunctions, so the terms that would come from
\(\partial\psi/\partial\lambda\) vanish. That is the Hellmann-Feynman
argument, and taking it one order up is the 2n+1 theorem, which is why a
\emph{third} derivative needs only first-order wavefunctions.

\textbf{Units are Rydberg atomic units throughout} (Ry, bohr), matching
QE. The only layer that speaks anything else is \texttt{io/} --- Hubbard
\texttt{U} in the \texttt{HUBBARD} card is in eV and is converted at the
boundary, as \texttt{pw.x} does it.

\textbf{Refuses:} \texttt{K\_POINTS\ gamma} selects the half-sphere
storage of the gamma-point trick, which is generated but not consumed
such a run is substituted by an explicit \texttt{k\ =\ 0} on the full
sphere --- the same physics at twice the storage --- and it says so.

\subsection{2. Ground state}\label{2-ground-state}

\begin{longtable}[]{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule\noalign{}
what & entry point \\
\midrule\noalign{}
\endhead
\bottomrule\noalign{}
\endlastfoot
self-consistent total energy, term by term & \texttt{run\_scf},
\texttt{calculation\ =\ \textquotesingle{}scf\textquotesingle{}} \\
the converged density, potential, wavefunctions & \texttt{SCFResult}
fields \\

\end{longtable}

\texttt{SCFResult} carries \texttt{total\_energy},
\texttt{energy\_terms} (QE\textquotesingle s own split),
\texttt{eigenvalues}, \texttt{occupations}, \texttt{wavefunctions},
\texttt{density}, \texttt{potential}, \texttt{fermi\_energy},
\texttt{homo}/\texttt{lumo}, and the magnetization fields when there is
one.

\textbf{Metals} are supported with every smearing QE has
(\texttt{gaussian}, \texttt{marzari-vanderbilt},
\texttt{methfessel-paxton}, \texttt{fermi-dirac}) and with the
tetrahedron family --- both the linear and the Bloechl-corrected forms
--- usable as an occupation scheme \emph{inside} the SCF, not only for a
density of states.

\textbf{Continuation across a change of spin regime}
(\texttt{starting\_from=}) promotes a converged state into a
target\textquotesingle s variables: non-magnetic into collinear,
collinear into noncollinear, spin-orbit switched on. It reaches the
\emph{same} self-consistent solution as a fresh run (≤4e-8 Ry on six
cases) in as little as one iteration. The magnetization is
\textbf{seeded} from the target\textquotesingle s
\texttt{starting\_magnetization} when the source has none, because
nothing in the SCF breaks spin symmetry on its own and an unseeded
promotion would converge back to the unpolarized solution and report
success.

\textbf{Convergence.} \texttt{mixing\_mode} selects \texttt{anderson}
(default), \texttt{linear} or Kerker/Thomas-Fermi preconditioning
(\texttt{TF}) \texttt{scf\_solver} offers a Newton-Krylov alternative to
mixing, which is slower but reaches solutions the mixer cannot hold.
\texttt{max\_iterations} defaults to 100 --- \textbf{a hard SCF may need
more}, and hitting the cap is reported as not converged rather than as
an answer.

\subsubsection{The equations}\label{the-equations}

The Kohn-Sham problem, generalised because ultrasoft and PAW make the
overlap non-trivial:

\[
\hat H |\psi_{n\mathbf k}\rangle = \epsilon_{n\mathbf k} \hat S |\psi_{n\mathbf k}\rangle , \qquad \hat S = 1 + \sum_{I,ij} q_{ij} |\beta_i^I\rangle\langle\beta_j^I|
\]

\[
\hat H = -\nabla^2 + v_{\rm loc}(\mathbf r) + v_{\rm H}[n] + v_{xc}[n] + \sum_{I,ij} D_{ij}^I |\beta_i^I\rangle\langle\beta_j^I|
\]

with \(n(\mathbf r) = \sum_{n\mathbf k} w_{\mathbf k} f_{n\mathbf k}
\big(|\psi_{n\mathbf k}|^2 + \sum_{ij} Q_{ij}^I \langle\psi|\beta_i\rangle\langle\beta_j|\psi\rangle\big)\),
the augmentation term being what ultrasoft adds. \(D_{ij}\) is rebuilt
from the potential every iteration, which is why the SCF is a fixed
point in \((n, D)\) and not in \(n\) alone.

Convergence is measured as QE measures it --- the Hartree energy of the
residual:

\[
dr^2 = \int\int \frac{\delta n(\mathbf r) \delta n(\mathbf r')} {|\mathbf r - \mathbf r'|} d\mathbf r d\mathbf r' < \texttt{conv\_thr}
\]

The DOS schemes live behind a name registry (\texttt{DOS\_SCHEMES}) ---
smearing and both tetrahedron variants --- and the projected DOS feeds
\emph{the same} registry as a per-band weight, so a PDOS and a DOS are
the same integration.

Projections are
\texttt{\textless{}phi\textbar{}S\textbar{}psi\textgreater{}} on
Löwdin-orthogonalised pseudo-atomic orbitals, resolved by atom,
\texttt{l} and \texttt{m}. Validated against a purpose-built
\texttt{projwfc.x} on seven cases: \textbf{6.9e-4} on a projection,
\textbf{4.7e-5} on a Löwdin charge, and the spilling parameter to all
four decimals it prints.

\subsubsection{The equations}\label{the-equations-1}

A band structure is one diagonalisation per k on a fixed density a DOS
is

\[
g(E) = \sum_{n\mathbf k} w_{\mathbf k} \delta(E - \epsilon_{n\mathbf k})
\]

with \(\delta\) replaced by a smearing function or evaluated by
tetrahedron interpolation. The projected DOS weights each term by the
overlap with an orthogonalised atomic orbital,
\(\ \big|\langle \phi_{\mu}|\hat S|\psi_{n\mathbf k}\rangle\big|^2\),
and the spilling parameter is what is \emph{not} captured:
\(\ \sum_{n\mathbf k} w_{\mathbf k} f_{n\mathbf k} (1 - \sum_\mu |\langle\phi_\mu|S|\psi\rangle|^2)\).

\subsection{4. Pseudopotentials and
functionals}\label{4-pseudopotentials-and-functionals}

\textbf{Pseudopotential kinds:} norm-conserving, \textbf{ultrasoft}, and
\textbf{PAW} --- the two-grid split, the augmentation charge, the
overlap operator, self-consistent \texttt{D\_ij}, and
PAW\textquotesingle s one-centre terms including its radial Poisson
solve and spherical quadrature. UPF v2 is read \textbf{UPF v1 is
refused}.

\textbf{Functionals:} LDA (PZ), \textbf{PBE}, \textbf{revPBE},
\textbf{PBEsol}, on the plane-wave grid and on the PAW spheres. The
functional is taken from the pseudopotential headers unless
\texttt{input\_dft} overrides it, and \textbf{an unimplemented one is
refused rather than silently replaced by LDA}. QE composes a functional
from four independently chosen slots and the UPF headers name all four,
so pypresso does the same.

Only the \textbf{energy} of a functional is written down \texttt{v\_xc},
and a GGA\textquotesingle s \texttt{v1} and \texttt{v2}, come from
\texttt{jax.grad} of it.

\textbf{Meta-GGA, potential-only branch:}
\texttt{input\_dft\ =\ \textquotesingle{}tb09\textquotesingle{}}
(Tran-Blaha) and \texttt{\textquotesingle{}bj06\textquotesingle{}}
(Becke-Johnson). These invert the rule above --- they \emph{are}
potentials, there is no energy --- so the consequences are enforced
rather than documented: \texttt{run\_scf} warns that its total is not
the value of any functional it minimised, and \textbf{forces, stress,
phonons and response all refuse}. Silicon\textquotesingle s gap goes
from LDA\textquotesingle s 0.49 eV to \textbf{1.13 eV} against an
experimental 1.17. Works with PAW, with noncollinear magnetism and with
spin-orbit coupling \textbf{plain ultrasoft is refused}, because it has
no partial waves to reconstruct \texttt{tau} from inside the sphere.

\textbf{Refuses:} energy-carrying meta-GGAs (TPSS, SCAN, M06L) --- their
potential has a \texttt{dE/dtau} piece acting on the wavefunction that
is not written EXX.

\textbf{Van der Waals:} Grimme \textbf{D2}
(\texttt{vdw\_corr\ =\ \textquotesingle{}grimme-d2\textquotesingle{}}).
Bilayer graphene matches \texttt{pw.x} to \textbf{3.1e-9 Ry} and binds
at 3.23 Å where PBE alone has no minimum. \textbf{D3,
Tkatchenko-Scheffler, MBD and XDM are refused by name} --- where
\texttt{pw.x} warns and silently runs with no correction at all.

\subsection{5. Spin, magnetism and
spin-orbit}\label{5-spin-magnetism-and-spin-orbit}

\begin{longtable}[]{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule\noalign{}
regime & how to ask & note \\
\midrule\noalign{}
\endhead
\bottomrule\noalign{}
\endlastfoot
unpolarised & default & \\
collinear & \texttt{nspin\ =\ 2}, \texttt{starting\_magnetization} & one
Fermi level, or two under \texttt{tot\_magnetization} \\
noncollinear & \texttt{noncolin\ =\ .true.} & magnetization is a vector
magnetic symmetry group \\
spin-orbit & \texttt{noncolin} + \texttt{lspinorb\ =\ .true.} &
\texttt{j}-resolved projectors, NC/US/PAW \\
\end{longtable}

\textbf{Three spin numbers are kept apart}, and the distinction is
exposed on \texttt{System}: \texttt{nspin} says which regime is in
force, \texttt{npol} is the number of spinor components of a
\emph{wavefunction}, and \texttt{nspin\_mag} the number of components of
a \emph{density}. They coincide at 1 and 2 and come apart at 4, where
\texttt{npol\ =\ 2} but \texttt{nspin\_mag} is \textbf{one} for a
nonmagnetic spin-orbit run.

\textbf{Magnetic fields and constraints:} a uniform field over the cell,
a field inside one atom\textquotesingle s sphere (through a
\texttt{LOCAL\_MAGNETIC\_FIELDS} card that \texttt{pw.x} has no
counterpart for), Elk\textquotesingle s \texttt{reducebf}, and all four
of QE\textquotesingle s \texttt{constrained\_magnetization} schemes.
\textbf{The field\textquotesingle s energy is not part of the reported
total} --- QE prints \texttt{etcon} and never adds it, and Elk excludes
its external field\textquotesingle s energy by the same convention both
numbers are carried separately.

\textbf{DFT+U:} QE\textquotesingle s \texttt{lda\_plus\_u\_kind\ =\ 0}
(Dudarev) with the \texttt{J0}/\texttt{beta} extension, on
\texttt{atomic}, \texttt{ortho-atomic} and \texttt{norm-atomic}
projectors, read from the \texttt{HUBBARD} card. Forces through the
Hubbard term come from differentiating through projectors that move with
the atoms. \textbf{Refused:} the full (Liechtenstein) formulation,
intersite \texttt{V}, background channels, the orbital-resolved variant,
the \texttt{wf}/\texttt{pseudo} projector sets, and noncollinear
\texttt{ns\_nc}.

\textbf{Refuses:} PAW + GGA + a noncollinear magnetization together
(\texttt{PAW\_gcxc\_potential} with \texttt{nspin\_mag\ =\ 4}).

wavefunctions, with the orthonormality constraint carried explicitly so
ultrasoft\textquotesingle s Pulay term is part of the same gradient.
QE\textquotesingle s \texttt{force\_lc}, \texttt{force\_cc},
\texttt{force\_ew}, \texttt{force\_us}, \texttt{addusforce} and
\texttt{force\_corr} are transcribed too, behind the same registry
(\texttt{force\_methods}) --- \textbf{as a cross-check, not as the
implementation}. The two share no machinery, which is what makes
disagreement informative it is how the augmentation
force\textquotesingle s sign and a missing gradient correction were
found.

Stress is the same construction in the other coordinate:
\texttt{sigma\ =\ -(1/Omega)\ dE/d(epsilon)}. \texttt{stress\_methods}
offers the analytic terms beside it note
\texttt{stres\_us}/\texttt{addusstress} are \textbf{not} transcribed, so
the analytic route offers terms and no total.

\textbf{Variable-cell} relaxes the cell and atoms together over
\texttt{3\ nat\ +\ 9} coordinates at an applied pressure, minimising the
\textbf{enthalpy} --- so a relaxed crystal carries the applied pressure
rather than having no stress. The gap between the frozen-basis energy
and a fresh SCF at the relaxed geometry is the Pulay error of the basis
and is \textbf{reported} (\texttt{VCRelaxResult.pulay\_error}) rather
than left to be noticed \texttt{treinit\_gvecs} rebuilds the basis per
step and makes it zero.

\subsubsection{The equations}\label{the-equations-2}

\[
\mathbf F_I = -\frac{\partial E}{\partial\boldsymbol\tau_I}\bigg|_{\psi\ \rm fixed}, \qquad \sigma_{ij} = -\frac{1}{\Omega} \frac{\partial E}{\partial \varepsilon_{ij}}\bigg|_{\psi\ \rm fixed}
\]

Both are one gradient. With ultrasoft the orthonormality constraint
\(\langle\psi_n|\hat S|\psi_m\rangle=\delta_{nm}\) is carried
explicitly, so its multiplier term --- QE\textquotesingle s Pulay
contribution --- falls out of the same derivative rather than being
added afterwards.

A variable-cell relaxation minimises the \textbf{enthalpy}, which is why
a relaxed crystal carries the applied pressure rather than having zero
stress:

\[
H = E + P \Omega, \qquad \frac{\partial H}{\partial h} = \Omega (P \mathbf{1} - \sigma) h^{-T}
\]

\subsection{7. Response: dielectric, phonons,
Raman}\label{7-response-dielectric-phonons-raman}

All of this is built on the \textbf{Sternheimer equation} --- a
projected conjugate- gradient solve per occupied band --- rather than a
sum over states. There are no empty states to converge and no division
by \texttt{eps\_n\ -\ eps\_m}.

The \textbf{perturbations are gradients of code that already exists}:
\texttt{dv\_of\_drho} is one \texttt{jvp} of \texttt{v\_of\_rho}, the
field\textquotesingle s commutator is the velocity operator,
\texttt{dvqpsi\_us} is one \texttt{jvp} through \texttt{at\_positions},
and \texttt{PAW\_dpotential} is one \texttt{jvp} of the one-centre
terms.

\textbf{Ultrasoft and PAW work} for the dielectric constant (≤1.2e-4
against \texttt{ph.x} on four cases). \textbf{Metals work} for the
response and the dynamical matrix. Phonons on a symmetry-reduced wedge
work as of the rank-\texttt{n} symmetriser.

\textbf{Refuses, by name:} Born charges for PAW (\texttt{int3\_paw}
against \texttt{becsumort} is missing, and the expression is wrong in
sign as well as size without it) \texttt{chi\^{}(2)} and the
electro-optic tensor (the second-order source term
\texttt{dvpsi\_e2}/\texttt{solve\_e2} has nothing to build the
\texttt{\textless{}u\_i\textbar{}r\_k\textbar{}u\_j\textgreater{}} piece
from --- and it is \textbf{42\%} of the answer, measured) the
non-analytic LO-TO term phonons at \texttt{q\ !=\ 0} the dynamical
matrix of an ultrasoft dataset and any response under a potential-only
meta-GGA.

\textbf{A trap worth knowing:} a response on a reduced k-set is a
\emph{polar vector field} and must be symmetrised as one. Running the
whole k-grid instead is only sound if the grid is closed under the point
group --- a \textbf{shifted} Monkhorst-Pack grid is not, and that
combination is refused by name.

\subsubsection{The equations}\label{the-equations-3}

Every first-order quantity comes from the Sternheimer equation --- the
linear response of an occupied orbital to a perturbation \(\Delta V\),
projected outside the occupied manifold so no empty states and no energy
denominators appear:

\[
\big(\hat H - \epsilon_n \hat S + \alpha \hat Q\big) |\Delta\psi_n\rangle = - \hat P_c^{+} \Delta V |\psi_n\rangle
\]

It is solved self-consistently, because \(\Delta V\) contains the
response of the potential to the response of the density --- see below
--- and the kernel \(K\) in it is one \texttt{jvp} of
\texttt{v\_of\_rho} rather than a second implementation:

\[
\Delta V_{\rm scf} = \Delta V_{\rm ext} + \int K(\mathbf r,\mathbf r') \Delta n(\mathbf r')  d\mathbf r'
\]

From its solutions:

\[
\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}^\infty = \delta_{\alpha\beta} + \frac{8\pi}{\Omega}\sum_{n\mathbf k} w_{\mathbf k} \langle \Delta_\alpha\psi_n | \partial_\beta \psi_n \rangle, \qquad Z^{\ast}_{I,\alpha\beta} = \frac{\partial F_{I\alpha}}{\partial\mathcal E_\beta}
\]

\[
C_{I\alpha,J\beta} = \frac{\partial^2 E}{\partial\tau_{I\alpha} \partial\tau_{J\beta}}, \qquad \omega^2 \mathbf e = \frac{1}{\sqrt{M_I M_J}} C \mathbf e , \qquad \frac{\partial \epsilon_{ij}}{\partial \tau_{I\gamma}}\ \ \text{(Raman)}
\]

The Raman tensor is the second-order energy differentiated once more
along a displacement, at \emph{frozen first-order} wavefunctions --- the
2n+1 theorem, which is why a third derivative costs one more
\texttt{jvp} and not a new solve.

Everything is built from one primitive --- the overlap of occupied
manifolds at neighbouring k-points --- because a determinant of overlaps
is blind to the unitary mixing a degenerate eigensolver leaves,
\emph{and} because the Fukui-Hatsugai-Suzuki lattice sum is an exact
integer on any mesh where a Riemann sum of a pointwise curvature is not.

\textbf{Z2 has two independent methods and running both is the check.}
Where they disagree the parity route is the answer, because it has no
mesh and the Wilson route does \texttt{WannierFlow.gap\_step} is the
Wilson result\textquotesingle s own diagnostic and is the number to read
before believing the integer.

\textbf{Two things bite in a plane-wave code and both are silent:}
neighbouring k-points do not share a G-sphere, so coefficients are
aligned by Miller index and the wrap at the zone edge is a \emph{shift}
of that index, without which the Chern number comes out smooth and
non-integer.

\subsubsection{The equations}\label{the-equations-4}

Everything is built from the overlap of occupied manifolds at
neighbouring k-points,
\(\ M^{(\mathbf b)}_{mn} = \langle u_{m\mathbf k}|\hat S|u_{n,\mathbf k+\mathbf b}\rangle\).
The Berry curvature is the phase of a plaquette of them and the Chern
number their lattice sum, which is an \textbf{exact integer on any
mesh}:

\[
F_{12}(\mathbf k) = \ln\big[ U_1(\mathbf k) U_2(\mathbf k{+}\hat 1) U_1(\mathbf k{+}\hat 2)^{-1} U_2(\mathbf k)^{-1}\big], \qquad C = \frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{\mathbf k} F_{12}(\mathbf k)
\]

\(Z_2\) has two independent routes --- the flow of Wannier charge
centres, and, when there is an inversion centre, the parity product over
the eight TRIM points:

\[
(-1)^{\nu} = \prod_{i=1}^{8}\ \prod_{n\ \rm occ}^{\rm Kramers} \xi_{2n}(\Gamma_i)
\]

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{9. Things with no \texttt{pw.x}
counterpart}{9. Things with no pw.x counterpart}}\label{9-things-with-no-pwx-counterpart}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Spin spirals} (\texttt{spiral\_q}, Elk\textquotesingle s
  \texttt{vqlss}) by the generalized Bloch theorem: the up component
  lives at \texttt{k\ +\ q/2} and the down at \texttt{k\ -\ q/2}, each
  on its own plane-wave sphere. In the rotated frame the density is
  lattice periodic, so the SCF, the functional and the mixer are
  untouched. \textbf{Refused:} spin-orbit coupling permanently (it
  breaks the theorem, and Elk refuses it too), symmetry until the spin
  space group is written (so a spiral needs \texttt{nosym}), and
  ultrasoft/PAW.
\item
  \textbf{Relaxing the spiral wavevector} (\texttt{relax\_spiral\_q}):
  \texttt{q} is a coordinate like an atomic position, \texttt{dE/dq} is
  \texttt{jax.grad} at frozen wavefunctions, and BFGS walks it downhill
  on the reciprocal metric. A magnetic field is refused here --- its
  energy is outside the reported total, so the state is stationary for a
  different functional than the one being differentiated.
\item
  \textbf{Per-atom magnetic fields} through a
  \texttt{LOCAL\_MAGNETIC\_FIELDS} card.
\item
  \textbf{Topological invariants} (§8).
\item
  \textbf{Rank-\texttt{n} tensor symmetrisation} ---
  \texttt{symme.f90}\textquotesingle s
  \texttt{symmatrix3}/\texttt{symtensor3} written at any rank.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{The spin-spiral
equations}\label{the-spin-spiral-equations}

The generalized Bloch theorem: a spiral of wavevector \(\mathbf q\) is a
spinor whose two components live on \emph{different} plane-wave spheres,

\[
\psi_{\mathbf k}(\mathbf r) = \begin{pmatrix} e^{i(\mathbf k - \mathbf q/2)\cdot\mathbf r} u^{\uparrow}(\mathbf r) \\[2pt] e^{i(\mathbf k + \mathbf q/2)\cdot\mathbf r} u^{\downarrow}(\mathbf r) \end{pmatrix}
\]

so in the rotated frame the density is lattice periodic and the SCF, the
functional and the mixer are untouched. Only two terms carry
\(\mathbf q\) --- \(|\mathbf k \mp \mathbf q/2 + \mathbf G|^2\) and
\(v_{\rm nl}\) --- and \(dE/d\mathbf q\) at frozen coefficients is
\texttt{jax.grad} of them.

\subsection{10. Performance: dials, memory and
GPUs}\label{10-performance-dials-memory-and-gpus}

\textbf{Two batching dials} control how much is in flight, and both
default to QE\textquotesingle s loop --- one k-point and one band at a
time, which is what a \emph{cache} wants:

\begin{Shaded}
\begin{Highlighting}[]
\VariableTok{PYPRESSO\_K\_BATCH}\OperatorTok{=}\NormalTok{all }\VariableTok{PYPRESSO\_BAND\_BATCH}\OperatorTok{=}\NormalTok{all }\ExtensionTok{python3}\NormalTok{ run.py}
\end{Highlighting}
\end{Shaded}

or per call, \texttt{run\_scf(...,\ k\_batch=...)}. \texttt{all} means
one \texttt{vmap} over the whole axis. The chunk size changes the order
contributions are summed in and nothing else --- round-off,
\textasciitilde1e-15 Ry.

\textbf{On a GPU both defaults are wrong.} A GPU has no cache to fit in
and inverts the conclusion: on a ten-atom metal, \texttt{k=1,b=1} costs
\textbf{4.5x} what \texttt{k=all,b=all} does on the same card. Set both.
The plausible half-measure \texttt{k=all,\ b=1} is the \emph{worst}
setting available, because it buys the batched mode\textquotesingle s
memory with the looped mode\textquotesingle s kernel launches.

\textbf{Measured GPU speedups} (one H200 against one CPU core, per SCF
iteration): 2x on a cheap metal, 3--5x on norm-conserving silicon,
9--21x with an augmentation charge or a magnetization, and
\textbf{83--115x on spin-orbit}. Energies agree to \textasciitilde1e-9
Ry --- the same agreements the CPU already has. See
\texttt{performance/gpu-sweep.pdf} and \texttt{PERFORMANCE.md}.

\textbf{Memory.} Spin-orbit is where it bites: a 20-atom bismuth cell
with a relativistic ultrasoft dataset peaks at \textbf{34.7 GB}, which
fits a 141 GB card and does not fit a 32 GB one. Everything else in that
sweep sits under 2 GB.

\textbf{Precision.} Every dtype comes from \texttt{config.Precision} x64
is enabled before any array exists, and all validation is float64.
float32 is a performance mode and \textbf{never one a correctness claim
is made in}.
